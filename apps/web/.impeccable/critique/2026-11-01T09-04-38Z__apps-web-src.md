## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment (A).** The system is authored; the page is generic. DESIGN.md is unusually disciplined for this size: one accent, a five-step olive ladder, twelve sizes with a stated ramp, hairline-only depth, an explicit no-kicker rule. Rendered it holds: 78px/780 display, hero lead 8.55:1, card body 6.76:1, 2px lime focus rings at 3px offset on every link and button. Real craft, not a template.

The composition is the problem. Copy-left / abstract-ornament-right hero, 2-up card grid, bordered panel with one form, display CTA: delete the words and this is any indie dev-tool landing page of the last three years. The orbit is the genre's most common abstract hero ornament and its two labels ("safe by design", "built to scale") are claims with no evidence, contradicting the project's own "no claim without evidence". Nowhere public does a visitor see what Booster Role actually does: no role chip, no permission list, no command, no verdict. The one unmistakably-this-product artifact — a booster role checked against the safety contract and returning "Safe to keep" — is behind a Discord login.

**Deterministic scan (B).** Source scan `impeccable detect --json src` (17 files): **exit 0, 0 findings**. Rendered-URL scan per route: `/` 4 (2 warning, 2 advisory), `/support` 1, `/privacy` 2, `/terms` 2, `/apps/booster-role/privacy` 3, `/apps/booster-role/terms` 4, `/dashboard` 0, 404 0. Rules:

## Overall Impression

The visual layer is the strongest thing here and it is not the bottleneck. What is missing is product proof and one vocabulary. The site is written in platform language ("workspace", "slug", "plan", "collection") for an audience that thinks in Discord terms ("my server", "boosters", "roles") — then asks them to log in through a paragraph in Indonesian. Biggest opportunity: put the booster-role safety verdict on the landing page, and let the dashboard be where you act on it rather than where you retype fixtures.

## What's Working

1. **The type system earns its mass.** 780 weight, -0.04em tracking, 0.96 line-height at 78px, exactly one Instrument Serif phrase per composition; holds at 390px where the h1 drops to 49px without reflowing into a paragraph.
2. **Contrast is real, not claimed.** Measured: hero lead 8.55:1, section body 7.3:1, card tagline 13.93:1, card body 6.76:1, orbit labels 7.92:1, footer 7.3:1. Zero contrast failures across 45 text elements on `/`. DESIGN.md's Text Tint Rule is true in the browser.
3. **States are written, not generated.** "No bots are published yet. The first one lands here the moment it ships." and the 404's "This page drifted away." are in the product's voice; 0 console errors and 0 failed resources on every route.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] The dashboard gate changes language at the highest-stakes moment
- **What:** `/dashboard` signed out: h1 "Your workspace." (EN) -> h2 "Sign in to manage this server" (EN) -> body "Login memakai Discord dan hanya membuka workspace kalau akunmu punya izin Administrator atau Manage Server di server ini." (ID) -> button "Continue with Discord" (EN). The three OAuth failure messages are Indonesian too, and the paragraph carries no `lang` attribute, so a screen reader reads Indonesian with an English voice.
- **Why:** the one screen where a stranger decides to hand over a Discord account. Half the sentence — the half explaining the permission check — is in a language the product never uses elsewhere.
- **Fix:** pick the shipped language and apply it here first; if Indonesian is the target, the whole surface needs it. If English: "Sign in with Discord. The workspace opens only if your account has Administrator or Manage Server in this server." Add `lang="id"` to any remaining foreign-language string.
- **Command:** `/impeccable clarify`

### [P1] The public lookup asks for a key the product never issues
- **What:** "Find your server workspace." -> field "Server slug" -> placeholder `demo-server` -> "Check server". A hit renders `Demo Server / Active · free workspace · <raw snowflake>`, a dead end. A miss renders "Server not found" and nothing else.
- **Why:** the panel's premise is recognition but it demands recall of a value no screen ever shows; `demo-server` is a dev fixture in production copy; the failure is unrecoverable (typo? not a customer? wrong key?).
- **Fix:** replace the slug with the real path ("Sign in with Discord and we'll show the servers you manage"), or keep it and name it what the user owns ("Server ID or slug"), keep the last result with a Clear control, turn a hit into `Open dashboard ->`, and rewrite the miss as "No workspace uses that slug. Check the spelling, or sign in and we'll find your servers."
- **Command:** `/impeccable clarify` then `/impeccable harden`

### [P1] The landing page never shows a bot
- **What:** the hero's right half is a 420px abstract orbit with two labels asserting quality and demonstrating nothing; the collection is two 550x242 cards of two sentences. No role, no permission list, no command, no verdict on the public surface.
- **Why:** Persuade has to make the product legible before it asks for a login; the ornament is the most expensive real estate on the page and it spends it on adjectives. The detector independently flags the italic-serif hero as the genre-default move.
- **Fix:** put the real artifact where the ornament is — `Booster Role · #c6f36b · position 4 · ManageRoles x · KickMembers x` under a `● Safe to keep` chip, i.e. the verifier's verdict as a still. Keep the rings if you love them; replace the ✦ core with the artifact. On the card, add the one line that makes it concrete: what a booster types and what they get.
- **Command:** `/impeccable bolder`

### [P1] The dashboard's main panel is a developer test harness wearing product clothes
- **What:** `RoleVerifier` renders nine text inputs (Discord user ID, role ID, Stored user ID, Stored role ID, role position, anchor position, comma-separated permissions) plus two checkboxes, all pre-filled with fixtures, then a Pass/Fail table with no `<th>` and no caption.
- **Why:** the contract's test fixture is exposed as the product's second half. An admin cannot obtain "Stored user ID" or "Anchor position" and cannot act on the outcome; it doubles the dashboard's control count and buries what they came for. The engineering idea is excellent — one authority for the rule, rendered not re-derived — but the surface is aimed at the person who wrote it.
- **Fix:** point the same contract at real data: pick a managed role, run `verifyBoosterRole` against it, show the verdict, the failing check, and the one action that fixes it. Keep the manual grid behind a "Diagnostics" disclosure. Give the table real `<th scope>` headers.
- **Command:** `/impeccable distill` then `/impeccable shape`

### [P2] Accessibility and responsive debt the polish pass left behind
- **What (measured):** form inputs signal focus only by moving a 1px border from `#3b4738` to `#c6f36b` (`outline: none`) — under the WCAG 2.4.11 focus-area threshold, while links and buttons get a proper 2px ring; three elements claim `aria-current="page"` on `/` (header brand, `Bots` fragment link, footer brand) and per-bot legal pages have none; legal tables scroll horizontally at 390px but the wrapper has no `tabindex`, so a keyboard user cannot reach the overflow; at 200% text zoom `/` overflows by 39px (`.nav-links`, `.nav-login`); at 320px every route overflows by 28px (`a.nav-login`); at 768px `/` overflows by 22px (`div.orbit-ring` absolute, right edge 780); legal `<th>` elements have no `scope` (7 in `src/legal/blocks.tsx:19`).
- **Why:** these are the parts a user cannot work around. Focus and table scrolling block keyboard users outright; the 320/768 overflow breaks the one-line-per-edge layout the system claims.
- **Fix:** give inputs the same 2px lime ring as everything else; set `aria-current` on exactly one element per page (and add it to the per-bot legal route); add `tabindex="0"` + `role="region"` + an accessible name to `.legal-table-wrap`; fix the nav wrapping at <=360px and the orbit's absolute geometry at 768px; add `scope="col"` to legal headers.
- **Command:** `/impeccable adapt` then `/impeccable audit`

## Persona Red Flags

**Jordan (first-timer, server owner).** Hero asks him to "Manage a server" before saying what managing means; he clicks, meets a sign-in card explained in Indonesian. He scrolls to "Find your server workspace", is asked for a "Server slug" he has never seen, types the only example he has (`demo-server`), and gets a raw snowflake back. He cannot tell whether he succeeded. Abandons at the lookup.

**Alex (power user, three servers).** Wants the guild list; gets one guild because `DISCORD_GUILD_ID` names a single guild, so the dashboard's own premise ("One dashboard for every server") is unreachable from the UI. No switcher, no shortcuts. The verifier interests him for four seconds, then asks for snowflakes he would have to fetch by hand — with a fixture already in the box. He leaves and uses the slash commands.

**Sam (keyboard + screen reader).** Strong start: skip link, `#main`, one h1, ordered headings, 6.7:1+ contrast, 2px lime rings on every link and button, 18 focusables with 0 unchanged. Then: the lookup input and all verifier fields have no visible ring (1px border only); the legal tables are unreachable by keyboard; three `aria-current` announcements on one page and none on the legal detail page; Indonesian copy unlabelled for language.

## Minor Observations

- Singular/plural drift: "Bot Collection" in the wordmark tag and `<title>`, "Bot Collections" in the product name and legal pages.
- Raw API strings as UI copy: a failed bots request renders `Unauthorized` as the entire section.
- "Ask about the waitlist ->" links to `/support`, which never mentions a waitlist.
- Measure drift: DESIGN.md claims a 66ch legal measure; rendered lines run ~88 characters (detector-confirmed).
- Five `straggler` one-word last lines across the site.
- Verifier fixtures (`100000000000000001`) read as real data to anyone who does not know they are fixtures.
- The deployed build still ships the five eyebrow kickers, the trust strip and "All rights reserved" that the worktree removed — nothing is committed yet.

## Questions to Consider

- If the orbit disappeared and the space held a real booster role with a `● Safe to keep` verdict, would the page lose anything?
- What is "workspace" doing in a product whose users say "server"?
- The verifier already knows how to judge a role — why is it asking an admin to type IDs instead of judging the roles the bot manages?
- If the dashboard could show one thing, would it be three member counts or one verdict with a fix button?
